01 Identify your anchor piece

Not write it. Identify it. The one piece that, if a stranger encountered it, would tell them you specifically are the person to follow on this topic.

ExampleWhat happens to a clean-label product in the supermarket supply chain
What do you know that almost no one else writing in your space knows? What's the part of your background that gives you a non-obvious vantage point?
Example11 years inside a national specialty grocery chain — watched the dynamics from inside the system
What specific detail, evidence, or experience would take someone else weeks of research to gather? What can you write in an afternoon that a competitor would need a month to match?
ExampleOperational detail across a half-dozen specific mechanics most public writing about this skips
What's the question your audience asks themselves at 11pm when they're confused or frustrated? Not the polished version — the messy real one, in their own words.
Example"Why does buying clean-label feel like such a compromise at the supermarket?"

02 Build your search intent map

10–20 phrasings your audience would actually type. Real questions, in their language — not yours. The questions, comparisons, and problem descriptions that surround your topic.

0 phrasings — aim for 10 to 20.
Examples"Why do clean-label products cost so much" · "What is a slotting fee" · "How does Whole Foods choose what to stock" · "Difference between organic and clean label"
03 Sketch your distribution sequence

One owned launch. Two or three earned placements that reach new audiences. One follow-up that builds on what you learn. Earned placements come from relationships cultivated months in advance — not cold outreach.

The owned launch

Where the piece publishes first. Your owned channel — newsletter, site, podcast — wherever your audience already consolidates and reads you.

ExampleThe Pantry Edit (Substack)
Example~1,400 subscribers who already follow my writing on this topic
The earned placements

Two or three placements that reach audiences new to you. Each one comes from a relationship in motion — not a cold pitch. Name the relationship state honestly.

ExampleGuest essay in an independent food publication · Cultivated and ready to pitch · Editor relationship cultivated over 4 months
The follow-up

The piece you'll publish in the weeks after the anchor piece, picking up a specific thread the original surfaces. Not part of the main launch — it builds on what the audience response teaches you.

ExampleThe Pantry Edit (the owned channel again)
ExampleWhichever question the comments and emails surface most — likely the reformulation question
Other relationship work in progress

Relationships you're cultivating that aren't yet tied to a specific placement. The bench you're building for the next anchor piece, not this one.

ExampleA podcast host whose newsletter I've engaged with for ~6 months; two writers at adjacent publications I've quoted and who've responded; a small owner Slack where my audience's actual questions surface.
